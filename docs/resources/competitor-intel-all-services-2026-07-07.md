## Source confidence model

- HIGH: official provider page, government/USAC page, or direct provider disclosure.
- MEDIUM: reputable news or third-party broadband publication. Use when official pages are address-gated or inaccessible.
- LOW: inference from mixed public evidence. Verify internally before live use.

## Universal verification rule

Do not quote any competitor price, speed, promo, eligibility, bundle, or equipment feature as final until the customer's actual address/account is checked. This is especially important because Spectrum, Xfinity, Cox, Optimum, T-Mobile, Verizon, AT&T, Astound, and GFiber all use address gating, market gating, promotional windows, or plan-specific eligibility.

Spectrum's own rate-card page states that residential services and rates vary by location, requires an address for accurate home information, and warns that not all products, pricing, and services are available in all areas; pricing and actual speeds may vary; speeds are based on wired connection.

## Executive intelligence summary

Spectrum's main competitive battlefield is not just download speed. It is:

1. Availability by exact address.
2. Total household telecom cost: internet + mobile + TV/streaming + voice.
3. Included equipment and Wi-Fi/security value.
4. Promo stability: 1-year promo vs 3-year/5-year/price-lock offers.
5. Reliability and customer support claims.
6. Upload speed symmetry: fiber competitors use this heavily against cable/coax.
7. Mobile-bundle savings: every major provider is using mobile as the household-cost lever.
8. Low-income/affordability paths: ACP is over, so providers now use proprietary assistance programs, Lifeline-adjacent criteria, or state/federal assistance proof.

Primary Spectrum threats:

- Google Fiber in San Antonio where available: simple pricing, symmetrical 1/3/8 Gig, included Wi-Fi, no data caps, no extra fees, Wi-Fi 7 on higher tiers, free install, strong local positioning.
- AT&T Fiber where available: symmetrical fiber, aggressive 1 Gig/5 Gig promos, unlimited data, All-Fi Pro/ActiveArmor value stack, 20% fiber-wireless cross-sell savings.
- T-Mobile Home Internet: low friction, 5-year price guarantee, no annual contract, included gateway, fast self-install, mobile bundle pricing.
- Verizon 5G/Fios: price guarantees, no equipment fees/data caps, up to $500 ETF switch credit, mobile-home bundle discounts, whole-home Wi-Fi add-ons, entertainment perks.
- Xfinity where available: 5-year price guarantee, unlimited data/equipment included on latest plans, free Xfinity Mobile line for 1 year, Peacock/StreamSaver value stack.
- Cox/Optimum/Astound in applicable markets: strong cable/fiber-coax bundles, mobile add-ons, Wi-Fi/eero/security positioning, price locks, ETF/money-back offers.

Spectrum counters:

- If fiber competitor is available: do not attack fiber. Shift to total household value, mobile savings, TV/sports/streaming, Spectrum coverage/service, no-contract framing, and current actual rate.
- If fixed wireless competitor is offered: emphasize wired broadband stability, speed consistency, gaming/work reliability, congestion risk, address-by-address serviceability, and Wi-Fi/equipment confidence.
- If competitor has price lock: compare full household cost, not just base internet. Ask about equipment, taxes, fees, mobile eligibility, promo expiration, installation, device balances, and channel/streaming needs.
- If customer is existing Spectrum and cites new-customer prices: acknowledge, review account, check mobile/TV/voice/value options, and avoid blunt 'new customers only' framing.

## Provider-specific deep notes

### Spectrum

- Phone balance payoff: up to $2,500 Visa Reward Card; Spectrum Internet required; 2-10 lines; transfer at least 2 numbers; latest bill required; $500/line, limit 5 lines.

Agent positioning:

- Lead with address verification.
- Separate Internet Advantage from Internet Assist.
- Use mobile as the biggest household-cost lever.
- Use switch/buyout for device-balance objections.
- For budget customers, Advantage may fit if available; for assistance customers, use Internet Assist workflow.

Threats:

- Fiber competitors beat Spectrum on symmetrical uploads.
- Xfinity/Optimum/GFiber beat with 5-year or no-shenanigans price framing.
- T-Mobile can beat on simplicity and low friction.

Counters:

- Household cost analysis, not single-line internet price.
- Mobile buyout and mobile line savings.
- TV/sports/local channels/streaming/Xumo/voice ecosystem.
- Wired broadband stability vs FWA.

## Competitive selling tactics by customer scenario

### Customer says: "I just want the cheapest internet."

Agent tactic:

- Ask usage and household size first.
- If light use: offer budget tier/assistance path if applicable.
- If multi-device household: show cost of underpowering.

Practice response:

I can keep this budget-focused. Before I pick the lowest plan, how many people and devices are usually online? If it is light use, we can check the budget tier or assistance path. If it is multiple people streaming, gaming, or working, I want to make sure we do not save a few dollars but leave the home frustrated.

### Customer says: "AT&T Fiber is better because uploads match downloads."

Agent tactic:

- Acknowledge fiber advantage.
- Verify address availability.
- Compare whole household: mobile, TV, security/Wi-Fi add-ons, install date, final price.

Practice response:

Fiber can be a strong option if it is live at your exact address. Let’s compare the complete package: internet price after promo, Wi-Fi/security add-ons, mobile savings, TV/streaming needs, and any device balances. Then we can see which option actually solves the household bill and performance problem.

### Customer says: "Google Fiber is simple: one price, fast speed."

Agent tactic:

- Do not attack GFiber.
- Use availability, install timing, total-service fit.

Practice response:

That is a strong offer where it is available. Let’s check whether it is live at this exact address and then compare what you need beyond raw speed: mobile, TV, streaming, home phone backup, installation timing, and final monthly cost.

### Customer says: "T-Mobile is cheaper and I can install it myself."

Agent tactic:

- Acknowledge simplicity.
- Compare FWA vs wired reliability.
- Ask about gaming, work, streaming, live TV, and congestion.

Practice response:

The self-install and lower entry price are real advantages. The question is whether fixed wireless will stay consistent for your home during peak times. If you work from home, game, stream live TV, or have many devices, we should compare consistency, congestion terms, and final price—not only setup speed.

### Customer says: "Xfinity includes equipment and unlimited data."

Agent tactic:

- Compare similar cable/fiber-coax packages.
- Ask about mobile line after promo, Peacock/streaming value, and full price after term.

Practice response:

That is a good bundle structure. Let’s compare final monthly cost after the included mobile line period, any streaming perks you actually use, and what mobile/TV/home phone would cost in your household. The best deal is the one that still works after the promo structure changes.

### Customer says: "Cox has Panoramic WiFi and security."

Agent tactic:

- Acknowledge Wi-Fi/security value.
- Ask about data cap, unlimited add-on, and mobile bundle requirement.

Practice response:

Panoramic WiFi and security can be useful. Let’s also compare the data allowance, whether unlimited data requires an add-on or mobile bundle, and what the final bill looks like with equipment, taxes, and fees.

### Customer says: "I owe money on phones. I can’t switch."

Agent tactic:

- Use Spectrum Mobile buyout path.
- Be precise: 2-10 lines, at least 2 numbers, latest bill, $500/line up to 5, max $2,500.

Practice response:

That may not block the switch. Spectrum has a phone-balance payoff path that can cover qualifying remaining device balances, up to $500 per line and up to $2,500 total. We need at least two transferred numbers, your latest bill, and Spectrum Internet eligibility. Let’s check whether your lines qualify.

## Agent flashcard candidates

1. What is the first competitive filter? Answer: exact address availability.
2. What is the second competitive filter? Answer: total household cost, not internet price alone.
3. What is the fiber competitor's strongest claim? Answer: symmetrical upload/download and reliability.
4. What is the fixed wireless competitor's strongest claim? Answer: low friction, self-install, and mobile bundle price.
5. What is the fixed wireless vulnerability? Answer: congestion/prioritization, service address limits, variable speed.
6. What is the cable competitor's strongest claim? Answer: price locks, included Wi-Fi/equipment/data, streaming/mobile promos.
7. What should never be said about a competitor price? Answer: that it is final without address/account verification.
8. What is Spectrum's best mobile objection-breaker? Answer: phone balance payoff up to $2,500 with strict eligibility.
9. What should agents do when ACP comes up? Answer: state ACP ended and move to current affordability workflow.
10. What is the difference between Internet Advantage and Internet Assist? Answer: Advantage is a budget plan surface; Assist is an affordability-program surface.
